# Editorial sequence

## 1. Lead story

The headline should name the day's actual contrast. The takeaway should interpret that contrast without explaining the calculation method.

Good: `대형주는 버텼지만 중소형주·기술주는 더 약했다`

Avoid: `오늘 시장에서 놓치지 말아야 할 변화`

## 2. Market map

Present SPY, QQQ, DIA, IWM, the Nasdaq regime, and the sector heatmap together. Add one short bridge sentence that connects the index comparison to the sector picture. Do not repeat the same regime state in multiple prominent cards.

## 3. Evidence-backed themes

Place themes before long stock analysis so readers understand the shared context first. Show only themes that passed the evidence threshold. Preserve uncertainty and do not fill a quota.

## 4. Movers and deep dives

Show the complete top/bottom ten table for breadth. Then provide a compact six-name overview grouped into:

- confirmed or supported catalyst;
- no confirmed single catalyst.

Put full prose, chart interpretation, and risk notes after that overview. This lets a reader stop after the summary without losing the main message.

## 5. Next-session checks

Close the editorial body with one to three unresolved questions or follow-up observations. This is a verification list, not a recommendation or forecast.

## 6. Appendix and knowledge links

Keep QA visible, but collapse or visually de-emphasize the full source list. Link to the OKF wiki for ETF families, glossary terms, historical timelines, and calculation methodology instead of repeating evergreen material in every daily report.
